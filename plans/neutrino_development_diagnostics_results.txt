Development timing/selection diagnosis, completed 2026-10-26

Main finding
------------
The learned arrival PDFs contain useful synthetic timing information that is
largely absent from the pooled hidden features. This is consistent with the
shared-context architecture averaging its temporal features before mixing
sensors. A low-false-alarm selection mismatch is also present, but correcting
that criterion benefits the raw/prior detector more than the learned detector
on this development cohort. It does not rescue the earlier detection claim.

These are development observations and plausible mechanisms, not a causal
architecture experiment or a fresh detector test. Earlier test results and
calibration outcomes are neither opened nor changed by this diagnostic.

1. Selection criterion
----------------------
The original balanced-validation-log-loss rule chooses L2=.1 for every head.
Reproduce the same grid at the same fitting budget and assess 20 repeated
two-fold background calibration partitions within validation. At nominal .01
per window and expected signal two:

Arm               L2=.1 mean eff.   Tail-preferred L2/eff.   Difference
Raw + prior           46.445%              1 / 49.785%        +3.340 pp
Added self-split      47.617%              1 / 48.320%         +.703 pp
Added untrained       47.793%             .1 / 47.793%         .000 pp

The raw/prior L2=1 point ranks first in every partition. The self-split L2=1
point receives .85 tie-adjusted first-place credit; L2=.1 receives .15.
The untrained L2=.1 point ranks first in every .01 partition. At nominal .001,
the self-split .1 choice remains preferred. The repeated partitions reuse the
same 256 two-photon signals, 128 source groups and 8192 null windows. Their
ranges are not confidence intervals, repetitions are not additional events,
and choosing the best validation point produces optimistic development results.
This is a reason to align future selection with the target metric, not a
confirmed performance gain. New test data would still be necessary.

2. Synthetic expected-timing retention
--------------------------------------
Fixed ridge probes are fit on positive TRAIN injections only and evaluated on
2048 positive VALIDATION observations from 128 sources. Signal-only expected
mean/q10/median/q90 times are computed from the known analytic source response.
Background has no signal-timing target. All 162 positive validation observations
with zero recorded photons remain included. No clean photon image is supplied
to encoder pretraining or fitting.

Primary fixed ridge penalty .1, MAE for expected signal mean time:

Input to timing probe                 Validation MAE (ns)
Constant training-target mean                 70.629
Raw + prior                                   63.550
Raw + self-split (full features)               63.006
Raw + untrained (full features)                63.900
Self-split PDF summaries only                  62.202
Untrained PDF summaries only                   70.331
Self-split pooled hidden features only         70.374
Untrained pooled hidden features only          70.118

PDF-only and hidden-only probes exclude raw/prior features. The learned PDF
retains a timing advantage over its untrained counterpart at every fixed
penalty (.01/.1/1); its mean-time MAE is 62.463/62.202/63.203 ns. Hidden-only
MAE is 70.219/70.374/70.517 ns, close to the 70.629 ns constant baseline.
These are point estimates from a limited linear probe, not uncertainty-qualified
parameter-reconstruction gains or proof of precise measured-timestamp retention.

At expected signal two, PDF-only gives 64.740 ns versus raw/prior 65.107 ns;
the improvement is small. For recorded counts 1--5, PDF-only is 66.920 ns versus
raw/prior 68.235 ns; above five it is 52.828 versus 54.649 ns. Empty observations
show no useful event-specific timing recovery: PDF-only 69.823 ns versus constant
68.204 ns. Empty-input predictions are priors, not newly observed evidence.

The timing target is a signal-only expected distribution summary under a known
fixed clock, whereas the original density objective predicts the complete
signal-plus-background photon location PDF. A timing-probe limitation alone
therefore does not establish that the density objective is mathematically wrong.

3. Pairing intervention
----------------------
Select 2048 validation windows by a fixed random permutation of row IDs, without
labels/counts/prediction-based selection. Within each recorded A/B half separately
shuffle the association of sensor IDs with time bins. Integer photon counts,
A/B membership, and BOTH per-half marginals are conserved exactly.

545 windows can change pairings; 377 actually change their joint count maps
(235 positive signal, 142 background). The other 1671 give zero PDF/hidden and
absolute feature-logit response, serving as a negative control.

On the 377 changed windows:
                                      Self-split             Untrained
Mean predicted-PDF total variation       .0141628             1.413e-7
Median relative hidden-feature L2       1.105e-8              2.716e-9
Mean relative hidden-feature L2         3.377e-5              3.707e-6
Mean absolute raw logit contribution      .136543              .151396
Mean absolute added logit contribution    .076109              .000764

The learned predicted distribution responds to joint sensor/time structure,
while the pooled hidden context is nearly invariant for most cases. The
learned-feature head does use its added features; it is not simply assigning
all of them zero weight. Sensitivity is not usefulness: static joint priors,
local nonlinear responses and response-processing assumptions can also affect
such logit changes. The intervention is not a physical neutrino injection or
a newly calibrated false-alarm comparison.

Cached original development features reproduce within numerical precision:
raw exactly, learned maximum difference 1.264e-5, untrained 1.789e-7. This
checks the rebuilt reference and saved encoder states before interpreting
the pairing responses. Numerical replay is distinct from a physical gain.

Execution correction and verification
-------------------------------------
The first diagnostic directory (development_diagnostics_v1) is preserved as
incomplete. Its stop came from reusing a production fitter that applies its
gradient gate to its selected candidate: the diagnostic inadvertently made
each candidate a selected one. Original learned/random L2=1e-4 points already
exhausted 300 iterations, with gradients .0002246/.0002120; neither was selected
in the completed detector probe. The v2 diagnostic retains all fixed-budget
points, labels their gate status, and excludes these failed points from tail
recommendations. No optimizer budget or original detector result was changed.

24 focused tests pass, including test/calibration access rejection, exact
marginal/count preservation, timing units, logit decomposition and retaining
unconverged candidates. Scoped Ruff passes. Selected original head scores
reproduce when refit; all data inputs match source head-lock digests.

Diagnostic computation takes 57.82 seconds before report rendering, peaks at
1.911 GiB process memory and .313 GiB allocated GPU memory. These are auxiliary
fits and frozen inference, not a measurement of encoder-training cost.

Interpretation and next action
------------------------------
First isolate the useful PDF features in a controlled classifier-head ablation
on development data. Use the same ablations for the untrained encoders, and
choose future heads for tail performance with adequate calibration precision.
The current hidden-only timing diagnostic does not prove that all hidden
features are useless for event detection; remove them experimentally rather
than assuming harm from their small timing signal.

Then, if needed, compare a dense global context that remains indexed by time
against a reference that averages time, at the same temporal resolution and
parameter budget. This is a candidate architecture change supported by the
diagnostic pattern, not a proven fix. Keep self-splitting/alternative-objective
controls matched and freeze a new protocol before fresh held-out evaluation.
Neither this development analysis nor any later tuning establishes recovery
of previously undetected real neutrino events.
