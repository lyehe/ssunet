Photon-splitting pilot: evaluation
Date: 2026-10-25
Historical scope: the 110-step checkpoint. A subsequent unchanged-architecture
5,000-step, three-seed pilot improves low-count results; see
plans/neutrino_longer_pilot.txt for the latest assessment.

Assessment
----------
The small dense model is affordable to train and uses information specific to
each event's retained photons. Its present prediction quality is worse than
a simple retained-count-plus-prior estimator, including in the intended low-
count regime. The pilot therefore passes the implementation/cost milestone;
it has not met the scientific success criterion in the original plan.

Evaluation scope
----------------
The original profile saved no checkpoint. Its exact seed-42 schedule was
reproduced: 10 warmup plus 100 profiled optimizer steps, approximately 2.2 passes
through 800 training events, using the original raw-count loss. Model weights
were retained for these diagnostics. Its frozen validation NLL reproduces the
original report within 1e-5 nats/photon. No network fitting used validation
targets, no test events were scored, and no truth sidecar was read.

Scores
------
Photon-weighted negative log likelihood; nats per held-out photon, lower better.
All-event validation: 100 observations, 55 with nonempty targets, 907 photons.
Low-count means 1--10 RETAINED INPUT photons: 50 observations, 36 with nonempty
targets, 120 held-out photons. It does not mean 1--10 total observed photons.

Fixed 50/50 retained-count/prior mixture          3.060        4.974

Model with count-matched donor inputs          [excluded]     9.530

The fixed mixture is q = 0.5*A/sum(A) + 0.5*original_training_prior, falling
back to that prior when A is empty. It uses no learned network or validation
bandwidth/mixture tuning. It was added as a posthoc diagnostic, not a previously
registered confirmatory test. Its score is better than the model by 1.625
nats/photon overall and 0.821 nats/photon in the low-count stratum.

Exploratory paired event/configuration bootstrap, 10,000 replicates:
  model minus fixed mixture, all:     +1.625 [0.602, 2.055]
  model minus fixed mixture, low:     +0.821 [0.225, 1.471]
  model minus matched donor, low:     -3.735 [-4.541, -2.750]
Negative model-minus-control differences favor the model. These descriptive
95% intervals condition on one trained network and the selected diagnostics;
they do not include model-seed, hyperparameter-selection or upstream latent-
configuration uncertainty. They are not a substitute for untouched-test results.

What the controls show
----------------------
1. Event conditioning helps. Twenty donor-input realizations replace each
   validation input with a training event matched on retained photon count.
   All low-count matches are exact; p, exposure and geometry are unchanged.
   Low-count model NLL worsens from 5.795 to 9.530. The result supports use of
   event-specific location/timing information beyond total brightness.
   All-event donor NLL is not interpreted: the five brightest validation events
   lack exact donor count matches, confounding that control outside low counts.

2. The initial baselines were weak. The original prior adds 7,680 uniform
   pseudophotons to 9,688 actual training photons, a 44.2% uniform component.
   Tuning only a training-derived time prior lowers its validation NLL from
   8.452 to 7.308. More importantly, even the fixed untuned retained-count
   mixture outperforms the model. The headline comparison against uniform or
   the original unconditional prior overstated the model's relative quality.

3. Global results are concentrated. One event supplies 47.6% of validation
   target photons, and five events supply 79.9%. The inverse concentration of
   photon weights, (sum B)^2/sum(B^2), is 3.81 effective weighted events. This
   is a concentration diagnostic, not a replacement sample-size estimate.
   Photon weighting is intentional and remains the primary objective; report
   retained-count strata and event-level uncertainty alongside it.

4. Empty-input prediction needs work. The model scores 9.285 with A=0, compared
   with 7.939 for the tuned prior. Only 11 events and 15 target photons support
   that comparison. Empty targets correctly contribute zero to a photon-location
   likelihood. These scores do not evaluate physical no-event detection.

5. Time coarsening is a partial limitation. The model reduces 256 time bins to
   64 before linear upsampling. Applying that operation to the selected simple
   estimator worsens its overall NLL from 2.689 to 3.490, consistent with loss
   of narrow peaks. However, its low-count NLL improves slightly from 4.696 to
   4.632. Coarsening alone does not explain the model's low-count deficit.
   This estimator diagnostic is not a causal architectural ablation.

6. The run is very short. No convergence or model-capacity conclusion follows
   from 110 optimizer steps. Train/validation learning curves and a fixed longer
   schedule are needed to separate undertraining from architectural limitations.

Exploratory baseline selection
------------------------------
The tuned prior selects among 36 candidates fit on training photons: Gaussian
time widths 0/25/50/100/200/400 ns and per-bin pseudocounts
0.0001/0.001/0.01/0.1/0.5/1. Its chosen width is 200 ns and pseudocount 0.0001.
The retained-count estimator selects among 144 candidates: six time kernels,
four spatial kernels (identity, 150 m, 400 m, uniform), and six prior weights.
The best all-event validation candidate uses 75% normalized retained counts and
25% tuned prior, with no time or spatial smoothing. Thus it is a count/prior
mixture rather than a nonzero-bandwidth smoother. Baseline choices were selected
on these scored validation observations, making the tuned scores optimistic;
the fixed mixture requires no such selection and reaches the same conclusion.

Cost and applicability
----------------------
Original profile: 3,585 parameters, approximately 955 training examples/second,
0.0793 GiB peak PyTorch tensor allocation on one RTX 4090. It measures only this
small 60-sensor model on a short, repeated-event pilot. It supports a practical
dense implementation; full-detector geometry and larger-model cost are unmeasured.

The physical reference is simulated P-ONE data in water. Upstream selection,
clock semantics, unit-photon interpretation and repeated latent configurations
still need full provenance review before a physical Poisson-supervision claim.
The exact analytic thinning audit remains valid for its constructed reference.
No result here establishes improved neutrino reconstruction or real-data benefit.
No photon ground-truth image, image PSNR or clean-image target is needed for
the reported held-out photon likelihood comparisons.

Recommended next experiment
---------------------------
Continue on the pilot before scaling to the 100,000-event comparison. Retain
checkpoints and train/validation curves for a fixed longer schedule. Keep the
fixed mixture and matched-count donor as required controls. Test a dense output
head that can preserve fine temporal structure and starts from, or explicitly
contains, the training-derived prior and retained-count mixture. Compare that
change with longer training of the current model so their effects are separable.
All gating/mixing choices at inference must use A, geometry and p only.

Proceed to the three-seed test study after model and baseline settings are fixed
on training/validation data. The scientific gate remains improvement over the
strongest simple control in the predeclared 1--10 retained-photon regime.
